A bounded structural simplification attempt — 4 November 2026
============================================================

Outcome
-------
A useful local organising principle emerged: normalize two completable
adjacent triples to a six-source core, and describe the remaining matching
requirements through small sets of outside witnesses. This gives a sharp
13-source statement, verified by two exhaustive programs, with a short
reduction to one nested configuration. We do not yet have an elementary
proof of that configuration. This is partial progress, not a substantially
shortened elementary proof of N5=15.

The production paper, Lean development and consolidation draft were not
changed. These are research artifacts, not formal verification. No novelty
claim relative to the literature is made.

1. Definitions and candidate consolidation theorem
-------------------------------------------------
Write C(S) for the directed cut S -> S^c on five colours, with |S|=2 or 3.
The two-element side is its pole pair. The three varieties in the class
have the same six tricoloured corners and three possible repeated colours.

A class with three sources has an own completion if, after reserving two
of those sources, the remaining sources match its six tricoloured corners.
The reservation concerns physical copies; their repeated colours do not
affect this six-corner condition.

Computationally verified statement, with the reductions below:

  Any pool of at least thirteen sources with two distinct triple classes,
  each having an own completion, contains a five-colour corner assembly.
  Twelve sources need not suffice, even with both own completions.

This strengthens the corresponding fifteen-source tools and treats pure
and mixed pools together. The remaining finite premise is described
explicitly below; it has not been replaced by an elementary argument.

2. Short mathematical reductions
--------------------------------
In a failure, each completable triple must contain all three possible
repeated colours: a same-repeat pair would supply the two bicoloured
positions and the own completion would supply the other six.

Repeat restoration: if another class has a cube with a repeat available
in A and a pole shared with A, reserve that cube and one A cube for an A
target's bicoloured corners. Start with A's own completion, restore the
second reserved A cube, and remove the other reserved source if it was
used. The restored A cube can replace it at any of A's six tricoloured
positions. This supplies the required assembly with distinct copies.

Consequently, two completable rainbow triples with intersecting pole pairs
solve immediately. With disjoint poles, relabelling and global reversal
leave just two cases: C(01), C(23), or C(01), C(014).

For C(01), C(23), use the opposite target 01|23. The triples supply three
sources for each directed half. Two outsiders filling one position in each
half complete it. If such two outsiders do not exist, all outsiders meet
only the same half: every source meets this target, and an outsider meeting
both halves together with any other outsider would suffice. Suppose that
half is 01 -> 23. Then the four reverse corners of C(23) are unavailable
outside that class, including on C(01). Its own completion after removing
two copies is impossible. The other half is symmetric.

Only the nested configuration remains.

3. The precise nested configuration
-----------------------------------
Let A=C(01), B=C(014), with one cube of each allowed repeat in each class.
This is a six-source core. Write

  F = {02,03,12,13}, alpha = {04,14}, gamma = {42,43}.

Thus A=F union alpha, B=F union gamma as tricoloured corner sets.

Elementary two-witness lemma:
  A has an own completion iff two distinct outsiders can fill two distinct
  A corners, at least one of which belongs to alpha.

For sufficiency, if the outsiders fill both alpha positions, the remaining
A and three B copies fill F. Otherwise the remaining A fills the second
alpha position and the three B copies fill the three remaining F positions.
For necessity, after reserving two A copies, the three B copies cannot
supply alpha. At least one alpha position therefore uses an outsider. If
the remaining A supplies the other, at least one F position also uses an
outsider; if not, both alpha positions use distinct outsiders.
The analogous statement holds for B and gamma.

In particular, both own completions are witnessed by at most four outsiders.
There need not be a single pair witnessing both: the core plus sources
18,18,21 in the indexing below is a counterexample to that stronger claim.

The finite statement verified here is stronger than needed:

  Add any seven sources to the six-source nested rainbow core. If some
  added source meets alpha and some added source meets gamma, the pool
  assembles. The two sources are permitted to coincide.

For thirteen sources this implies the two-completion theorem immediately.
For larger pools retain the core, a witness meeting each pair, and enough
other copies to reach thirteen. No hypothesis is lost. Thus the extension
to larger pools does not require a further inventory search.

4. Exhaustive verification and sharpness
---------------------------------------
The C++ search enumerates sorted seven-source extensions, pruning a branch
once its current pool assembles. Solubility is monotone under adding sources,
so this pruning preserves every failing extension.

An independent Python program reconstructs the 75 corner sets directly;
it imports neither the supplied research model nor the C++ compatibility
table. It repeats the search with augmenting-path matching, checks every
failed terminal pool against all 255 Hall subsets of each target, and checks
own completions against all 63 subsets of the six requirements.

It visits 706 terminal thirteen-source pools after pruning and finds exactly
ten failures. Every one lacks an added source meeting alpha, or lacks an
added source meeting gamma. None has both own completions. The complete
lists and search counts are in two-loops-independent.json. This is exhaustive
finite verification in the directed-corner model, not a hand exhaustion or
a new proof of the physical-model correspondence.

A twelve-source failure is:
  one each of the three varieties in C(01),
  one each of the three varieties in C(014),
  three C(04) sources repeating 1,
  two C(14) sources repeating 0,
  one O(1;02|34) source.

It has both own completions. Only thirteen target varieties are even
corner-supported. Each has a displayed deficient set in
  twelve-source-witnesses.txt,
which also gives the two explicit six-corner matchings. This is a directly
checkable counterexample, not merely a failed search for an assembly.

5. Why the most tempting broader reductions did not work
--------------------------------------------------------
We tested a strong direct replacement principle: replace k actual adjacent
sources by m virtual opposite sources so that every assignment of distinct,
physically co-occurring target corners to the virtual sources can be lifted
to distinct actual sources. This is sufficient for transferring arbitrary
assemblies, but more restrictive than a construction that may retarget.

Results:
  3 adjacent -> 2 opposite: none, over all 1,540 adjacent class multisets.
  4 adjacent -> 3 opposite: none, over 80 symmetry orbits.
  5 adjacent -> 3 opposite: seven of 273 symmetry orbits work; every allowed
    output consists of three identical opposite varieties.

The final 4->3 and 5->3 tests use only corner triples occurring on an actual
target (820 distinct triples), avoiding an overly strong arbitrary-triple
requirement. The negative results therefore concern the stated physical
assignment-lifting condition. They do NOT exclude all possible compression,
replacement or retargeting arguments. The successful 5->3 cases do not
satisfy the multiplicity cap of the existing opposite-source theorem.

A second tempting simplification also fails. After repeat restoration, the
nested core permits eight adjacent outsider varieties and all fifteen
opposite varieties. No pair from these 23 types solves the six-source core.
Thus an immediate pair-conflict graph cannot carry this part of the proof:
interactions among larger groups are essential.

Finally, separate own completions cannot always be witnessed by the same
pair. The common-witness probe found 76 unsolvable minimal examples with
separate witnesses and no common pair. The four-witness bound above remains
valid; the proposed two-witness bound for BOTH completions does not.

6. Assessment and bounded next target
------------------------------------
The thirteen-source theorem could consolidate the pure and mixed
' two own-looped triples ' arguments, with fewer ambient hypotheses and a
sharp boundary. The nested two-witness formulation is a useful elementary
organising lemma in its own right.

However, the exhaustive nested-core check is not yet an elementary proof.
It does not remove the concentration reductions, the private-column work,
or the separate three-opposite constructions. No defensible page reduction
has been measured. We should not replace those proofs by a claim that the
new computation has made the whole argument elementary and short.

The focused mathematical question is now:
  Prove directly that seven outsiders meeting both private pairs alpha and
  gamma complete the nested rainbow core.

A short geometric proof could justify replacing a cluster of existing
lemmas. A lengthy classification of all seven-source extensions would
repeat the problem we are trying to avoid. Do not expand this into another
unbounded case analysis merely to recover the computational result.

For the present paper the recommendation remains unchanged: retain the
compact certified corner proof and the structural/cuboid mathematics. This
attempt gives a specific candidate simplification, not evidence that the
154-page elementary dependency chain has become a practical primary proof.
