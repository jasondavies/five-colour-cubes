Three witnesses for two nested completions
=========================================
Status: a written elementary argument. The local incidence audit is
supplementary; this proof does not assume N5=15, the fourteen-failure
classification, or the thirteen-source computational result. No Lean proof
has been added.

Let A=C(01) and B=C(014). A pool contains three specified physical copies
from each class; their repeated colours can be arbitrary. Call these six
copies the core and all other copies outsiders. Suppose that each class
has an own completion: after reserving two of its specified copies, its
six tricoloured corners can be matched to distinct remaining sources.

Claim. Either the pool assembles an opposite target, or at most three
outsiders, together with the core, retain both own completions.

Notation
--------
Use directed complementary pairs for tricoloured corners. Set

  F = {02,03,12,13},  alpha = {04,14},  gamma = {42,43}.

Then A=F union alpha and B=F union gamma.

First observation: two witnesses suffice for either completion
------------------------------------------------------------
The own completion for A exists precisely when two distinct outsiders can
occupy two distinct A corners, at least one in alpha.

Indeed, after reserving two A copies the core has one A and three B.
If the outsiders fill both alpha corners, those four core copies fill F.
If they fill one alpha corner and one F corner, the A fills the other
alpha corner and the three B fill the remaining F corners.

Conversely, any completion uses an outsider at an alpha corner. If the
remaining A fills the other alpha corner, at least one of the four F
positions uses another outsider, since only three B copies remain. If A
does not fill the other alpha corner, that corner uses a second outsider.
The same assertion holds for B with gamma in place of alpha.

Second observation: any outsider meeting F can be used in either witness pair
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Let W be such an outsider. If another outsider X meets alpha, W and X
form an A-witness pair, assigning W to F and X to alpha.

If no other outsider meets alpha, W is the only outsider available at
alpha. The existence of an A-witness pair from the first observation
then forces that pair to contain W. Thus in both cases W belongs to an
A-witness pair. The identical argument places W in a B-witness pair.

The two pairs share the same physical W, so their union has at most three
physical sources. This proves the claim whenever an outsider meets F.

If every outsider avoids F, there is an explicit assembly
-------------------------------------------------------
No opposite source avoids F. Its underlying four-cycle would otherwise
lie in the graph obtained from K5 by deleting the four edges between
{0,1} and {2,3}. That graph is the two triangles 014 and 234 sharing only
vertex 4, and has no four-cycle.

For an adjacent source C(S), avoiding F means that if S contains 0 or 1,
it must contain both 2 and 3. Since |S| is two or three, the only possible
outsets are

  23, 234, 123, 023, 24, 34.

Their intersections with alpha and gamma are:

  outset       alpha       gamma
    23          empty       empty
   234          empty       empty
   123          {14}        empty
   023          {04}        empty
    24          empty       {43}
    34          empty       {42}

Since all outsiders avoid F, an A-witness pair must fill both alpha
corners. Thus both C(123) and C(023) occur. Likewise the B-witness pair
forces both C(24) and C(34).

Choose one source from each of those four distinct classes and assign

  C(123):20,  C(023):31,  C(24):21,  C(34):30.

They fill the reverse half of opposite target 01|23. Three specified A
copies and one specified B copy fill its forward half, for example

  A:02,03,12;  B:13.

All eight sources are distinct. This is an opposite-target assembly,
proving the alternative and completing the argument.

How this fits the general two-completion question
------------------------------------------------
In a failure, an own-completed triple uses all three repeat colours.
The repeat-restoration argument excludes intersecting pole pairs for two
such triples. Disjoint cuts with disjoint poles give an opposite assembly
directly. The only remaining arrangement is the nested one above.

Therefore any failing pool with two own-completed triple classes has a
subpool of at most nine sources retaining both triples and both completions.
This is a locality statement, not an assembly theorem: small subpools with
both completions can fail, and extra sources still have to be used to
prove the thirteen-source bound.

A common pair of witnesses is not always available. For outsiders P,P,Pbar,
where P=C(04) and Pbar=C(123), an A-witness pair needs Pbar and one P, whereas
a B-witness pair needs both P copies. All three are needed to retain both
completions. This explains why identifying the two pairs would be invalid.

Audit
-----
check_three_witnesses.py independently constructs the twenty cut classes
and fifteen opposite types. It tests all 1,426,425 four-source multisets.
Precisely 81 have the two witness pairs but no three-source witness union:
one source from each of C(123), C(023), C(24), C(34), with the 3^4 possible
repeat choices. All 81 satisfy the displayed opposite assembly.
The proof above explains this exhaustion without relying on that test.
